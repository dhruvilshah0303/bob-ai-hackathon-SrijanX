# Problem Statement

## Background

Emergency medical dispatch is a time-critical coordination problem. An ambulance destination must account for the patient's suspected condition, treatment capability, available beds, specialist coverage, and travel time.

## The Problem

A nearest-hospital rule can be clinically wrong when the nearest facility has no ICU capacity, lacks the required specialist, or has a slower route under current traffic. Dispatchers need a fast decision that combines those constraints and explains the recommendation clearly enough to trust and act on.

## Who Is Affected

Emergency dispatchers and ambulance crews make the decision. Emergency department coordinators and hospital operations teams are affected when capacity is not reserved consistently or when an incoming patient is not pre-alerted.

## Why It Matters

A delayed or unsuitable destination increases time to treatment, creates avoidable rerouting, and can consume scarce capacity that another ambulance needs. The operational cost is measured in minutes, coordination workload, and preventable clinical risk.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Distance-only routing optimizes the wrong objective. Manual phone calls and separate dashboards make it difficult to compare hospitals consistently, reserve the last available bed, or understand how a capacity change affects an ambulance already in transit.
